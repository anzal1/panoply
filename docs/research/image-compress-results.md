# Image compress, resize to exact KB and budget results

Measured on 2026-10-14 on an Apple M5 Max (18 cores) running macOS, with the image corpus from
`tests/corpus/image.toml` (67 files, fetched and generated by `tests/corpus/fetch.py` and
`generate.py`). Everything here is reproducible:

```
cargo run --release -p panoply-image --example compress_report /tmp/compress-tables.md                        # tables in section 1 (prints them too)
cargo run --release -p panoply-image --example kb_proof                                                  # table 4
cargo build --release -p panoply-bench -p panoply-cli
target/release/panoply-bench run --module image.compress --runner cli \
  --cli-args "run image.compress {input} --opt animation=first --out {out_dir}" --kind image --runs 3 --verbose
```

The release build used for the bench had thin LTO and 16 code generation units (the shipped profile is
fat LTO with one unit, which is a few percent faster), so speed numbers are slightly pessimistic.

## 1. Compress: size at the same picture quality

"Same picture quality" is one definition for every contender: SSIM of luma and of chroma, the worse of
the two, at least 0.97 (`panoply_image::ssim`, the Panoply default for `target = smallest`). Each
contender's quality setting was searched for the smallest file that still scores 0.97 on the same
decoded pixels, and each was also run at its own defaults. Cell format: bytes (similarity, quality).

Contenders: Panoply `image.compress` (the module, through the library), MozJPEG at its defaults (the
`mozjpeg` crate, which is the code `cjpeg` from MozJPEG runs: progressive, trellis, optimized tables,
quality 75), libjpeg-turbo `cjpeg` 3.2.0 (the `cjpeg` on this Mac is not MozJPEG), `cwebp` 1.6.0 and
oxipng 10 at its defaults. No MozJPEG command line tool and no oxipng command line tool is installed here,
so those two ran as libraries.

Reading the table:

- At the same similarity Panoply and a MozJPEG quality search are the same size to within 0.1 percent,
  which is expected: Panoply drives the same encoder and its search finds the same quality. The value
  Panoply adds over "MozJPEG at its defaults" is the targeting. Quality 75 is smaller on 3 of the 13
  pictures only because it misses the 0.97 bar there (similarity 0.923, 0.950 and 0.954), and larger than needed
  on the others (the sharpest example, `ultra-hdr.jpg`, is 227,840 bytes at 0.992 against 72,686 at 0.971).
- Against libjpeg-turbo `cjpeg` at 0.97 Panoply is 12.6 percent smaller over the 13 pictures. Against
  `cwebp` at 0.97 it is level overall (cwebp wins on some pictures and loses on others), and it stays JPEG.
- The search stops at quality 10 for JPEG, so a very flat picture can score above 0.97 and stay larger than
  a quality 4 encode would be (`exif-gps-orientation6.jpg`: 5,148 bytes at 0.987 against 4,093 bytes
  at 0.976). Panoply also keeps the color profile by default, which is why `display-p3.jpg` carries about
  2.5 KB more than the bare encode.
- `dog-extended-xmp.jpg` is large (12 MP) and already a quality 91 JPEG: the saving is 32 percent.

## 2. Resize to exact KB: every preset inside its range

Every preset that publishes a size limit (15 shipped, 14 with a limit) on 20 varied photos: 8 EXIF
orientation variants of one landscape, three gain map JPEGs, a 12 MP phone portrait, a 14 MP panorama, a
31 MP wide shot, a 227 by 149 thumbnail, a 302 by 403 portrait with orientation 5, a phone portrait with GPS
and orientation 6, Display P3, CMYK, and a PNG with profile and metadata.
Each preset and photo was fitted to the preset's pixel size once and solved three times: with the
preset's own reading of KB (or the size that holds under both readings when the portal does not say),
then forced to 1000 and to 1024 bytes per KB. Each result is checked against the published numbers by
plain arithmetic in the example, not by the module's own window: at least `min x 1024` and at most
`max x 1000` for "both readings at once", and `min x base` to `max x base` for a stated base.

**840 cases (20 photos), 0 outside the window, 81 needed padding, 144 reduced in pixels, 84.9 s**

The `Padded` column counts results that needed the last-resort comment padding to reach a minimum
(`rrb-signature` asks for 30 KB at 140 by 60 pixels, which no real picture reaches by itself; the card
says so every time). Presets without published pixels (`pan-utiitsl-photo`, `rrb-photo`, `upsc-photo`,
`pan-utiitsl-signature`) are solved in free size mode: the picture keeps its shape and shrinks only as far
as a JPEG quality of 75 needs, which is why 144 cases report "reduced in pixels".

## 3. Budgets over the image corpus

`panoply-bench run`, release build, three runs per file, through the `panoply` command line
(`--runner cli`), 67 files per module including the broken and unsupported ones (still measured, still
held to the memory cap). Peak memory is the peak physical footprint of the process, not resident size
(ImageIO maps files and caches that count toward resident size but not footprint: the 200 MP HEIC shows
900 MB resident and 115 MB footprint).

| module | files with an error | wall p50 | wall p95 | peak MB p50 | peak MB max | first preview (median, files up to 10 MB) | speed against a native tool (median, worst) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| image.convert | 9 | 29 ms | 434 ms | 10 | 1991 | 28 ms (slowest 2320, 200 MP HEIC) | 1.24 x `sips`, worst 7.9 (200 MP HEIC) |
| image.compress | 13 | 125 ms | 5758 ms | 17 | 2204 | not run (not an instant transform) | not run (no native tool does a similarity search) |
| image.resize | 9 | 28 ms | 516 ms | 15 | 1913 | 23 ms (slowest 3477, 200 MP HEIC) | 1.06 x `sips`, worst 4.1 (400 MP PNG) |
| image.resize_to_kb | 9 | 80 ms | 572 ms | 6 | 1905 | 74 ms (slowest 3963, 200 MP HEIC) | not run (no native tool) |
| image.strip_metadata | 11 | 5 ms | 9 ms | 3 | 26 | 5 ms (slowest 12 ms) | 0.69 x `jpegtran -copy none` on 19 JPEGs, worst 1.6 |

The errors are the files that should fail, with plain messages: `truncated.jpg`, `truncated.gif`,
`corrupted.heic`, an arithmetic-coded JPEG, the 17000 by 17000 AVIF that ImageIO cannot read in bands,
SVG and an unsupported TIFF flavor, plus (compress only) the pictures over 24 megapixels, which are sent
to Resize. Strip refuses GIF, ICO, EXR and JPEG 2000 (no metadata container support).

What the five budget rows say, honestly:

| budget | convert | compress | resize | resize_to_kb | strip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak memory under 300 MB | FAIL on 4 files | FAIL on 5 files | FAIL on 4 files | FAIL on 4 files | PASS (max 27.6 MB) |
| First preview under 100 ms | median passes, FAIL on the big HEICs | not measured | median passes, FAIL on the big HEICs | median passes, FAIL on the big HEICs | PASS |
| Speed at least as fast as a native tool | FAIL (median 1.24 x, tolerance 1.10) | not measured | median 1.06 passes, worst file fails | not measured | median 0.69 x `jpegtran`, FAIL by the harness on one truncated JPEG (1.6 x) |

The memory failures are four kinds of file and nothing else:

- Camera RAW through ImageIO (three files: about 2.0 GB, 1.3 GB and 0.3 GB for 12.2 MP, 11.2 MP and 4.5 MP
  sources; up to 2.2 GB in Compress).
  ImageIO offers no tiled RAW path. The Convert spec already carries a RAW exception ("about 8 bytes per
  pixel", tiled demosaic is the target); measured it is far above that. This needs either an own demosaic
  or ImageIO's reduced-size RAW decode for jobs that shrink the picture, neither built.
- `guitar-rotated-cw90.hif`, a 33 MP Canon HIF with 3520 by 1600 tiles at 10 bits: about 800 MB inside
  ImageIO whatever we do, because one tile is the smallest unit it decodes
  (`crates/panoply-platform-macos/src/lib.rs`). Compress refuses it as over 24 MP.
- Compress of 12 MP HEIC photos (`apple-depth-portrait.heic` 359 MB, `display-p3.heic` 348 MB): the
  hardware HEVC encoder and decoder keep memory per tile for the life of the process; a single
  conversion to HEIC is 258 MB and the search adds the verification decode.
- Everything else is under the cap: the 400 MP PNG converts in 20 MB, the 120 MP JPEG panorama in 25 to 65
  MB, the 200 MP HEIC in 99 to 111 MB, and the largest Compress input (a 23.5 MP baseline JPEG) in 257 MB.

The speed numbers for Convert and Resize use the fast JPEG profile (baseline with optimized tables, plain
PNG deflate). With the full MozJPEG profile Convert was 3.7 times slower than `sips` (median) and Resize
1.4 times; the full profile makes JPEG files 9 to 28 percent smaller at 10 to 12 times the encode time, so it
is kept for Compress, where size is the job.

## 4. Things the measurements found and fixed along the way

- Compress peaked at 410 to 670 MB on 14 to 31 MP JPEGs. Causes: three copies of the picture, a whole
  decoded copy for verification, and a clone inside the JPEG encoder call. Now one copy, verification in
  bands of 128 rows, and no clones: 259 MB at 14 MP, 257 MB at 23.5 MP, and a plain refusal above 24 MP.
- Free size Resize to exact KB used to take any quality that fit, down to quality 1 at 4000 pixels. It now
  shrinks until quality 75 fits.
- Under a size stopped a few percent short because of whole quality steps and a cap at the source's own
  quality. Sixteenth steps through MozJPEG's luma table, and no cap for a size the person asked for.
- Lanczos3 rang by about 4 percent of an edge's height. Output is now limited to the range under the kernel's
  main lobe: a 64 to 192 edge that came out as 59 to 193 now comes out as exactly 64 to 192.
- HEIC and AVIF written by the OS encoder lost EXIF and GPS while the card said they were kept. The
  source's ImageIO property dictionaries are now carried.
- "kb_basis = both" did nothing for presets that state a basis; it now forces the size that holds under both.
